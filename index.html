No bad data, just a bad learner

We fine-tune a series of pretraining checkpoints of one language model on training data whose labels are partly or entirely wrong, and watch what happens to accuracy on clean held-out data.

The question is whether wrong labels are bad in themselves, or whether what they do depends on how far along the learner is. Every experiment takes checkpoints saved at different points of the pretraining of the same model, fine-tunes each of them on the same task with a chosen fraction of wrong labels, and measures accuracy against the true answers as the fine-tuning goes on.

What a training example looks like

The model reads the first part and is trained to write the second. In this example the label is wrong on purpose.

How to read the figures

Each line is one pretraining checkpoint, coloured from dark for the earliest to light for the latest and averaged over seeds. The dotted line of the same colour marks where that checkpoint stood before fine-tuning. A line that ends above its dotted line belongs to a checkpoint that is more accurate on clean data after fine-tuning than before; a line that ends below belongs to one that lost accuracy.
